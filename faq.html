1. Accessibility Standards & Core Concepts

Why is manual screen reader testing necessary if we already run automated accessibility scanners?
Automated testing tools (such as Axe-core, WAVE, or Google Lighthouse) identify approximately 30% of WCAG compliance failures—chiefly programmatic syntax checks like missing `alt` attributes or basic color contrast ratios. However, automated scripts cannot verify whether keyboard focus order is logical, whether custom modal dialogs trap screen reader cursors, or whether dynamic asynchronous alerts are announced properly. True usability can only be verified through 100% manual testing with real assistive technology operators.
What is the difference between WCAG 2.2 Level AA and Level AAA?
WCAG 2.2 Level AA is the recognized legal standard for commercial websites, ADA Title III compliance, and Section 508. Level AAA is the highest, most stringent tier of digital inclusion. It mandates enhanced color contrast (7:1 for normal text vs 4.5:1 in AA), strict 24x24 pixel touch targets, error prevention across all transactional inputs, and cognitive assistance. Resilience Industries Limited specializes in helping enterprise platforms attain full Level AAA conformance.
What are the new success criteria introduced in WCAG 2.2?
WCAG 2.2 officially introduced nine critical success criteria focusing on cognitive load, low vision, and motor accessibility. Key criteria include: (1) Focus Appearance (SC 2.4.11), (2) Focus Not Obscured (SC 2.4.12 & 2.4.13), (3) Target Size Minimum (SC 2.5.8), (4) Redundant Entry (SC 3.3.7), and (5) Accessible Authentication (SC 3.3.8 & 3.3.9). Our audits validate every single one of these criteria.

2. Auditing Methodology & Laboratory Testing

Which screen readers and operating systems do you test with?
Our physical testing laboratory evaluates platforms across real hardware devices using: (1) Google TalkBack 14+ on Android, (2) Apple VoiceOver on iOS/macOS, (3) NVDA (NonVisual Desktop Access) on Windows paired with Chrome and Firefox, and (4) Freedom Scientific JAWS 2024 on Windows. All desktop evaluations are performed using physical hardware keyboards with zero mouse assistance.
What deliverables will our development team receive after an audit?
Your team receives: (1) An Executive Compliance Brief summarizing legal exposure and risk scores, (2) A Granular Issue Dossier mapping each violation to its exact URL, DOM selector, and WCAG SC, (3) Production-ready HTML5/ARIA remediation code snippets, and (4) Empirical screen-recorded video proofs demonstrating friction points as experienced by screen reader users.
Do you provide post-remediation verification after our developers fix the bugs?
Yes. All Standard WCAG Audits and Enterprise VPAT packages include one complimentary regression testing round. Once your engineering team applies the provided code patches, our laboratory re-tests the impacted user journeys and issues an updated Compliance Certificate.

3. VPAT® & Legal Accessibility Reporting

What is a VPAT and why does our enterprise need one?
A Voluntary Product Accessibility Template (VPAT®) is an internationally standardized template created by the Information Technology Industry Council (ITI). When completed by an independent auditor, it becomes an official Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR). U.S. Federal agencies (Section 508), state governments, universities, and enterprise procurement departments require a valid ACR before approving software procurement bids.
Can our own developers write our VPAT ACR in-house?
While vendors can technically complete their own VPATs, self-authored reports are heavily scrutinized and often rejected by federal buyers due to inherent bias and lack of empirical assistive technology validation. An ACR authored and signed by an independent accredited accessibility specialist carries legal credibility and satisfies procurement review boards.
How long is an Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR) valid?
An ACR represents a point-in-time evaluation of a specific software release or website version. As long as your core user interface does not undergo major redesigns, an ACR is generally accepted for 12 to 18 months by procurement officers. If major architectural changes occur, an audit delta check is recommended.
